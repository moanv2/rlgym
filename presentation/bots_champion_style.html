Bot 1 · Marian · the basics

What a basic bot is rewarded for

  • 🏎️
    Chase the ball

    Reward for moving toward it and facing it.

  • 🎯
    Drive it at the goal

    Reward for pushing the ball at the opponent's net.

  • 🥅
    Score, don't concede

    Big plus for a goal, equal minus for conceding.

stage_1_basics.json
{
  "zero_sum": true,
  "velocity_player_to_ball": 0.10,   // chase
  "face_ball": 0.05,   // face it
  "velocity_ball_to_goal": 0.30,   // at the net
  "event": {
    "goal": +1.0,   // score
    "concede": -1.0    // don't
  }
}

These few signals already produce a bot that reliably chases the ball and drives it at the goal. Everything that follows builds on them.

DefaultObs · 89 512 × 3 ~1.35B steps

The trade-off
A richer reward needs more training to pay off.

With enough timesteps this reward could refine into a very strong bot. Within the project's time limit, a simpler scheme trained faster and played better.

Bot 4 · Marco · adversarial

Train against tougher opponents

A bot that only plays itself just learns to beat itself. Marco froze the team's bots and trained to counter them. His win rate against each jumped.

vs Diego
50% → 81%
vs Nachi
30% → 61%
vs Martin
18% → 45%
Average est. tournament
~44% → ~67%
Obs transplantReused a 2.0B brain, widened its inputs 89 to 107.
Low learning rateDropping to 3e-5 broke a plateau.

These are Marco's own matchups against the bots he trained to beat, so they flatter him. In the full tournament he lands 3rd. The gains still show the method works.
