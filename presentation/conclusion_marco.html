05 · CONCLUSION

What we built, what we learned

From a random network to a champion — the six things that mattered.
01 · FROM ZERO
Self play, no demos
Became the champion with no human examples and no scripted moves.
02 · SCALE WON
Compute was the ceiling
Ten billion steps on a 1024-wide net. More training, not cleverer tricks, won it.
03 · REWARD DESIGN
Shaping taught the habits
A zero-sum reward turned a ball-chaser into a bot that scores and defends.
04 · OBSERVATIONS
Relative beat absolute
AdvancedObs (107) trained faster and read the play better than DefaultObs (89).
05 · CURRICULUM
Each generation lifted the next
Each bot kickstarted from the previous best, then surpassed its teacher.

05 · LIMITATIONS & NEXT

Where it stops, where it goes

The honest limits, and the roadmap if we kept going.
Limitations where it stops
×
1v1 only. No teammates, no team play yet.
×
Self play only. It never faced a human or another team’s AI.
×
CPU bound. Compute, not ideas, was the ceiling.
×
Reward shaping is finicky. Hand tuned across v4 to v7.
×
Not deployed. It lives in the simulator, not the real game.
If we continued where it goes
→
Deploy with RLBot and play real Rocket League.
→
Benchmark against humans and public bots.
→
Scale to 2v2 and 3v3 with attention over teammates.
→
More compute, longer runs, bigger networks.
→
Automate reward search instead of hand tuning.
the simulator is solved  ·  the real game is next
